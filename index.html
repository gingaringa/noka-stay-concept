    05

    The hard parts
    are ours.

    Renting a home in Japan generally means a guarantor, a contract in Japanese, and a two-year term. If your family is not here and your stay is measured in months, that shape does not fit. Closing that gap is the reason a service like this exists.

    Provisional copy — terms to be confirmed

    01
    A guarantor you do not have Most Japanese leases need a joint guarantor — usually a relative, usually living in Japan.
    A stay, not a tenancy You arrive as a guest, not as a tenant signing a two-year contract. The house is accommodation, so the paperwork a lease demands is simply not part of it.
    02
    A contract in a language you are still learning Lease documents are difficult for Japanese people too.
    In English, in writing Everything explained before you agree to any of it. Terms, costs, and what happens if your plans change. In a language you actually read.
    03
    Two years, when you have six months A digital nomad visa runs six months and cannot be extended.
    Built for months A place designed for long stays, not for nights. The length of your stay is a conversation, not a fixed term you have to fit into.
    04
    A door that quietly does not open Some landlords still decline foreign tenants. It is rarely said outright.
    Ours opens You are not being screened for your passport. We built this for people arriving from somewhere else. That is the whole point of it.

    Proposed support model

    Provisional

    The things nobody warns you about are usually small, and they are usually solved in one message.

    01

    The rubbish calendar, in English, on the fridge. Which day, which bag, and what to do with the net.

    02

    A clinic that will see you without Japanese. We know which ones, and we will call ahead.

    03

    The delivery slip you cannot read. Photograph it, send it to us, choose a new time.

    04

    School, childcare, a dentist, a haircut. Ordinary questions get ordinary answers, same day.

    05

    If you decide to stay longer, we help you find the next address. Some guests arrive for two months and start looking for an apartment in the fourth. That search is the other half of what we do.

    06

    And if Mizue is wrong for you, we will say so. If you need to be in Shibuya at nine every morning, this is not your address. We would rather tell you on a call than take a deposit.

    08

    Nothing is booked yet.
    Let's talk first.

    Thirty minutes, in English, with the person who will actually be looking after your stay. No deposit, no obligation, no sales script.

    What we will ask you

    01When you are arriving, and roughly how long you want to stay
    02Who is coming with you — alone, a partner, children, parents
    03Where you need to be during the week, and how often
    04Your visa situation, so we can tell you what is and is not possible
    05Whether you are thinking about staying beyond this trip

    What happens after

    →We send you what we talked about, in writing, in English
    →You take as long as you need. Nothing is held, nothing expires
    →If you go ahead, we start the paperwork. If you don't, that is fine too

    No deposit is taken on this call and nothing is booked. If Mizue is not right for you, we will say so and, where we can, point you somewhere that is.
