How to take the rubbish out in Edogawa-ku without getting it wrong.

It is the first thing that will make you feel like you are failing, and the first thing that will make you feel like you live here.

On your second morning you will look out of the window and see a neat pile of translucent bags on the corner, under a blue net weighted with bricks. There will be nobody near it. By nine o'clock it will be gone, along with the net, and the corner will look like nothing ever happened there. This is the part of Japanese daily life that no guidebook covers, because it is not interesting until it is yours.

Edogawa-ku sorts household waste into four streams, and each one has its own day. Burnables go out on Tuesday and Friday. Non-burnables go out twice a month. Recyclables — bottles, cans, PET — go out on their own day, and cardboard and newspaper on another. The days differ by chome, which is why asking a neighbour who lives two streets away can give you a confidently wrong answer.

The three rules that actually matter

First, put it out in the morning, not the night before. Collection starts at eight; bags left overnight attract crows, and crows are extremely good at their job. Second, use the right bag — transparent or translucent, so the collectors can see what is inside. Opaque supermarket bags will be left behind with a sticker on them. Third, put the net back. The net is communal. Somebody folded it and left it weighted at the corner, and that somebody is now, occasionally, you.

Nobody will explain the net to you. One morning you will simply fold it, and that will be the morning you stopped being a visitor.

If you get it wrong, nothing dramatic happens. A small printed sticker appears on your bag explaining, in Japanese, which rule you missed, and the bag stays where it is until the correct day. It is not a fine. It is closer to a note from a teacher. Most people get it wrong twice and then never again.

What we do about it

Guests at the house get the collection calendar for this chome in English, on the fridge, with the bag types and the corner marked on a small map. If a sticker appears on your bag, send us a photograph and we will tell you what it says. It is a small thing. It is also, reliably, the first thing anyone asks us about.
